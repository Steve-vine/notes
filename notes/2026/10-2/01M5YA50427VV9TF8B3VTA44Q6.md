## What the ADR decides

- **A vulnerability is one issue on one system.** A system is a technology asset.
  - "OpenSSL 3.0.7 (CVE-2023-0286)" on three servers of the same system is one vulnerability, with three places listed.
  - The same issue on two systems is two vulnerabilities, each with its own owner and fix-by date.
  - The register can group by issue to show "this CVE is on 12 systems".
- **Reference `VUL-12`**, numbered per company.
- **Kind:**
  - **Software flaw** (a CVE or similar);
  - **Misconfiguration** (CSPM-style);
  - **Code weakness**;
  - **Exposed secret**;
  - **Other**.
- **Severity:**
  - Critical, High, Medium, Low, Informational;
  - with a CVSS score and vector when the source gives one;
  - comes from the source, and can be changed by a person with a reason;
  - informational items are kept, but hidden from the default view and the figures.
- **Sources and sightings.** Each source that reports the issue on the system is a *sighting*: which source, first seen, last seen, the source's own reference and severity. Two sources reporting the same issue on the same system are one vulnerability with two sightings. "Same issue" means the same CVE, or for non-CVE issues the same source check.
- **States:**
  - **Open**;
  - **Fixed**: by the source no longer seeing it (sprints 92–93), or by a person for hand-entered and pen-test items;
  - **Accepted**: with a reason and a review date, and optionally a decision record. It reopens when the review date passes;
  - **False positive**: with a reason;
  - **Logged in error**: hand-entered only.
  - Raising a risk links the vulnerability to the risk register and leaves it open.
  - Seen again after Fixed, it reopens as the same VUL, and the history shows it came back.
- **Fix-by date** = first seen + the target for its severity, from a rubric (Admin ▸ Rubrics). It is overdue after that.
- **Owner:** by default the system's owner, and can be changed. An owner who holds no internal role works their vulnerabilities in the User Portal, as asset owners do (ADR 0072 §10).
- **Nothing is deleted.** It is audited like the other registers. Each day's figures are kept as a snapshot (the ADR 0070 pattern).
- **Unmatched sightings:** a sighting Compass can't put on a system waits in an Unmatched list until a person or a rule places it (sprint 92).
- **Relation to other modules:**
  - a pen test is an SAP testing entry, and its report is evidence;
  - accepting with a decision uses decision records;
  - Inventory's findings become one source (sprint 93).

## Who can do what

| Permission | Allows |
|---|---|
| View vulnerabilities | See the module and the register |
| Work vulnerabilities | Record, assign, change severity, mark fixed, false positive, raise a risk |
| Accept vulnerabilities | Accept a vulnerability as a risk (a sign-off; kept apart so it can be held by fewer people) |
| Manage vulnerabilities | Settings, sources' company mapping and matching rules (sprint 92) |

Built-in Admin only. The starter roles stay frozen.
