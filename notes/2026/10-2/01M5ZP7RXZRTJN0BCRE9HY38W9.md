Challenge raised 27 Oct 2026. Steve's money, Steve's call; this is the sized view the plan needs. Not regulated advice.

**What is held**
- SIPP £55,874 + ISAs £29,856 (HL): 100% in MSTR (Strategy Inc), a company whose share price is effectively a leveraged bet on bitcoin. £85.7k = 38% of all invested assets, 100% of the accessible/flexible money.
- Workplace pension £137,762 (Smart Pension): 100% LGIM Future World North American Equity Index. Diversified across ~500 US companies but 100% equity, one region, one currency, no de-risking as Steve approaches drawdown.

**Why it matters for this plan specifically**
- The plan draws ~£40k/yr from the pots between Steve 62 and 69 (scenario A). Sequence risk is highest exactly then. MSTR has fallen 70–90% from peak more than once (2022: ~−75%). A fall of that size in 2030–32 removes roughly £60k+ of today's £86k and with it most of the ISA money that bridges the gap years.
- The model assumes 2.5% real growth on everything. That is a reasonable planning number for a diversified portfolio. For MSTR the expected outcome is unknowable and the range is enormous in both directions. A plan cannot rely on it; it can only size it so that the plan survives it going to zero.
- The North American fund is a defensible growth holding but the pension is the largest asset and has no glide path. A 2008-style −40% year at 59 would push scenario A below the £45k target.

**Questions to settle (task CAR-14)**
1. What share of invested assets is Steve willing to treat as speculative, i.e. could go to zero without changing the retirement date? Common practitioner rule: 5–10% of net investable assets. Today it is 38%.
2. Where does the rest go? Inside the SIPP and ISAs, switching is free of CGT and only costs HL dealing fees (£11.95 per trade). A global all-cap equity index fund or a global multi-asset fund are the usual diversified homes; HL charges 0.45% on funds versus a capped £200/yr (SIPP) and £45/yr (ISA) for shares, so cost rises slightly.
3. In the workplace pension: whether to move from North America only to a global index, and whether to start a gradual glide toward some bonds/cash from ~57–58 so the first 3–5 years of gap-year drawdown are not exposed to a crash. Check what Smart Pension offers and the total charge (member fee £1.75/month plus fund and platform charges, to confirm).

**What I am not saying.** Not that bitcoin exposure is wrong, and not that timing matters. The point is sizing: the retirement plan should be funded by the diversified money, and the speculative money should be an amount whose loss would be annoying rather than plan-changing.
