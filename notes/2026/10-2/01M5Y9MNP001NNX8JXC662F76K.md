## What Steve saw (2026-10-27)

On Compliance, Test Company shows **384 Implemented and 1 Not implemented**. It should show 384 Implemented and 1 Not applicable.

The one is PDH.19, the new UK GDPR Art. 27 control (COM-1345). Steve pressed the **Not applicable** Status button on its assessment.

## Cause (checked on staging)

An assessment has two separate ways to say "not applicable":

- the **"In scope"** switch at the top, which asks for a reason when switched off;
- a **"Not applicable"** button among the four Status buttons.

Nothing ties the two together. PDH.19 is saved as Status Not applicable with In scope still **on**, and it is the only assessment on staging in that state.

Every figure follows the switch, not the button. A control still in scope whose Status says Not applicable is counted as **Not implemented**: on Compliance's bar, the headline percentage, the Dashboard and the framework figures. This is deliberate in the code (`core/posture.control_state`), but nothing on screen says so.

ADR 0018 meant the Not applicable status to be "set when applicable = false". The form never enforced it. The API (`AssessmentUpsert`) and Polaris's `posture_update_assessment` accept the mixed state too.

## Behaviour (proposed, for Steve to confirm)

1. **One way to say it.**
   - The Status buttons offer Implemented, Partial and Not implemented.
   - A control is made Not applicable only by switching **"In scope"** off and giving the reason.
   - While it's out of scope, Status reads "Not applicable" and its buttons are hidden.
   - Switching it back in scope asks for a status again.
2. **Saved answers in the mixed state are not quietly changed.** Opening one shows a note: "Marked Not applicable but still in scope, so it counts as Not implemented. Switch In scope off and say why, or pick its status." It stays counted as before until someone does one of those. No figure moves without a reason being recorded.
3. **The same rule for Polaris and the API.** Saving Status Not applicable while in scope is refused with a plain message pointing to "In scope". It's refused the same way as an out-of-scope save without a reason (COM-800).
4. **Unchanged:** Compliance, the Dashboard and the frameworks count an out-of-scope control as Not applicable, and it leaves the denominator.

Alternative, if Steve prefers: keep the Not applicable button, and make pressing it switch In scope off and ask for the reason. Same result with one more path to it.

## Related, not in this task

Taking PDH.19 out of scope does not move UK GDPR's 65/66. That needs Art. 27 itself ruled out of scope on the framework page (see the COM-1345 correction comment).
