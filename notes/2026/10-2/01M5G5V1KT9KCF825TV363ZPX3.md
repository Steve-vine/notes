Sprint 83, Risk Assessments (ADR in COM-1219). Steve's decision: load GDPR as a framework this sprint.

**Chain:** independent (seed data; no migration, no API change) — can be built in parallel with the chain.

## What people see

- **UK GDPR** in Frameworks, beside SOC 2 and ISO 27001, and offered when choosing a company's frameworks.
- Requirements: the articles a controller or processor must meet — **Art. 5–11** (principles, lawful basis, consent, children, special categories), **12–23** (rights), **24–43** (controller and processor duties: by design, processors, records of processing (30), security (32), breach notification (33/34), **DPIA (35)**, **prior consultation (36)**, DPO (37–39), codes and certification), **44–49** (international transfers) — at article level, with paragraph-level requirements where an article holds several distinct obligations (e.g. 33(1) 72 hours / 33(5) breach log).
- Each mapped to the core controls (the privacy domain PDH.*, plus security, vendor, incident and records controls) with how much of the requirement it covers (ADR 0056), so the coverage gauge and SoA-style views work as for the other frameworks.
- The mapping notes say where a module carries it: Data Subject Requests (Art. 12–22), Incidents (33/34), Inventory data assets (30), Risk Assessments DPIA (35/36).

## Notes (technical)

- Source: UK GDPR text on legislation.gov.uk (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence v3) — titles and short paraphrases; note the licence in a .SOURCES.md like HITRUST's.
- Follow the "adding a framework" checklist: five test files pin counts/rules; test_crosswalk.py is a unit test — run `pytest -q` with no -m.
- EU GDPR is near-identical; one framework, UK GDPR, with a note.
