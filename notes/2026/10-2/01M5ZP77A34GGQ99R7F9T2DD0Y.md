Analysis 27 Oct 2026, from Steve's figures. Planning view, not regulated advice.

**Facts.** Mortgage £100k at 3.99% fixed to July 2028, £1,420/month, term ends July 2033 (Steve 62), overpayments up to £10k/yr during the fix, anything after. Household surplus ~£1,000/month during the renovation. Steve's pension is salary sacrifice (Smart Pension), 8% + 4%, no matching above 4%, no bonus. Taxable pay after sacrifice is £101,200: £1,200 of it sits in the 60% effective band (personal allowance taper).

**The comparison, per £1 of take-home given up**
| Route | What £1 of net pay becomes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Extra salary sacrifice, first £1,200/yr | £2.63 gross in pension (60% tax + 2% NI) | Escapes the taper band. Do this regardless. |
| Extra salary sacrifice beyond that | £1.72 gross in pension (40% + 2% NI) | Taxed ~15% on the way out → £1.47 net, before any growth. Locked until 57 (Jan 2028). |
| ISA | £1.00 | Tax-free out, fully flexible. Lesley's £20k allowance is unused. |
| Mortgage overpayment | £1.00, earning a guaranteed 3.99% | Scenario G (overpay £10k/yr) clears the mortgage at 59 instead of 61 but leaves the affordable income unchanged at £51k: at these rates it is neutral versus investing and clearly behind pension. |

**Recommendation to test with payroll**
1. Raise salary sacrifice by £1,000/month gross (to ~17% employee). Net cost ≈ £560/month; the first £100/month costs only £38. Pension contributions rise from £13.2k to £25.2k/yr gross, within the £60k annual allowance.
2. Put the remaining ~£440/month into a stocks-and-shares ISA in Lesley's name first (unused allowance), in a diversified fund (see the concentration memo).
3. Do not overpay the mortgage during the fix. Revisit in spring 2028 before the July 2028 fix ends: if the remortgage rate on offer is above ~6%, or if being mortgage-free at 60 matters more than the arithmetic, repay then (any amount is allowed after July 2028). The ISA money is the natural source.
4. Ask Moneypenny whether any of the 15% employer NI saving on sacrificed pay is passed on (some employers add it to the pension). From April 2029 only £2,000/yr of sacrificed contributions is NIC-exempt, so the NI edge shrinks then; the tax relief does not.

**Carry-forward.** Only ~£13.2k of the £60k allowance was used in each of 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26, so roughly £140k of unused allowance is available until it rolls off. Useful if a lump sum (e.g. from reducing a concentrated holding outside a tax wrapper) ever needs a home; contributions are also capped at 100% of earnings in the year.

**Caveats.** Pension money is taxed on the way out and, from April 2027, unused pension counts for IHT on second death (spouse exempt). The ISA is the right home for anything that might be needed before 57 or in the 60–67 gap years when flexibility matters. When the renovation finishes the surplus should rise; re-run the split then.
