## Control Objective

Employee agreements and workplace policies are established, maintained, and enforced to define the terms and conditions of employment, outline behavioural expectations, and ensure the responsible use of company resources across Callitech Limited's Wrexham (UK), Atlanta (US), and Florida (US) offices.

## Implementation Guidance

Employment contracts – All personnel are issued a written contract, tailored to comply with UK employment law (Wrexham) or applicable US federal/state employment law (Atlanta, Florida), covering job responsibilities, compensation, work schedule, and employment status. Contracts include confidentiality, data protection, and intellectual property clauses reflecting the employee's exposure to PII, call recordings, CRM data, and customer content handled in Callitech's telephone answering and live chat operations. Contracts are signed before the employment start date and stored securely in the HR system with access restricted to HR personnel.

Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) – Defines acceptable and prohibited use of company systems including CRM, telephony/call-recording platforms, email, internet, and mobile devices. The AUP explicitly addresses handling of PII, call recordings, and customer content, and prohibits export or local storage of cardholder data or client data subject to HIPAA obligations, consistent with the company's self-certified PCI DSS status and HIPAA commitments for relevant clients. Employees acknowledge the AUP at hire and upon material revision.

Employee handbook – Covers employment terms, leave, workplace behaviour, a code of conduct, disciplinary procedures, and security expectations. Distributed to all staff across all office locations, reviewed at least annually, and acknowledgment tracked in the HR system.

Clean desk practices – Given the contact-centre handling of PII and call recordings, workstations must be locked when unattended and sensitive material (paper or digital) secured; periodic walkthrough checks are performed at office locations.

Remote/hybrid working – Where roles are approved for remote or hybrid arrangements, secure connectivity (e.g., VPN, MFA) and company-approved devices are required, and expectations are documented and communicated to eligible staff.

Code of conduct – Establishes expectations of integrity, respect, confidentiality, and non-discrimination consistent with UK and US legal requirements, and addresses conflicts of interest and harassment. Acknowledged by employees and contractors at hire and refreshed periodically.

Accountability and disciplinary process for control responsibilities

Assignment of control responsibilities – Security, privacy, and confidentiality responsibilities relevant to each role (for example, correct handling of PII, call recordings, CRM data, and customer content; adherence to the AUP; completion of assigned awareness training; timely reporting of suspected incidents) are set out in employment contracts, the handbook, and role descriptions, and are communicated to employees and contractors across the Wrexham, Atlanta, and Florida offices.

Evaluation of adherence – Adherence to these responsibilities is considered as part of the periodic performance review process for all staff, including managers, who are additionally accountable for the control responsibilities of their teams. Repeated failures such as non-completion of mandatory training or recurring policy breaches are recorded in the review record and reflected in development or corrective action plans.

Formal disciplinary process – A documented disciplinary process applies to violations of the code of conduct, AUP, or other security and privacy policies. HR, in consultation with the Head of Infrastructure and Cybersecurity where the matter is security- or privacy-related, investigates reported violations and determines the response proportionate to severity and intent — ranging from coaching and formal warnings to suspension of system access, termination of employment or contract, and, where warranted, referral to regulators or law enforcement. The process is applied consistently in a manner compliant with UK employment law (Wrexham) and applicable US federal/state employment law (Atlanta, Florida), and provides for escalation to executive management and, for material matters, notification to the Board.

Reporting without retaliation – Employees and contractors may report suspected violations, ethical concerns, or control weaknesses through their manager, HR, or the security function; the company prohibits retaliation against individuals who report concerns in good faith.
