The other half of COM-555. Steve's two accounts landed in two different companies, and neither of them chose to.

**Today the choice belongs to the browser, not to you.** The selected company is kept in one local-storage slot on the machine. Sign out and it stays. Sign in as somebody else and it stays. Open a second browser, an incognito window or another laptop and there is no choice stored at all, so you silently land in the default company.

That gives two wrong behaviours from one cause:

- **A fresh session drops you somewhere arbitrary.** Not where you left off — wherever the default company happens to be. This is what made a request disappear: the account that raised it was in one company, the account that went looking was in another, because it had never been asked.
- **Two accounts on one machine inherit each other's company.** Sign in as somebody else on a shared or test machine and you pick up the last person's selection. Harmless while it is Steve twice; less so when it is an assessor who thinks they are looking at their own client.

## Decision to make

**Proposed: the company follows the account.** Remember it against the user on the server, restore it at sign-in, and update it when they switch. Then a colleague who works in one company always opens in it, on any machine, and signing in as somebody else never inherits a stranger's context.

The alternative is to keep it per browser but scope the stored key to the signed-in account and clear it on sign-out — cheaper, fixes the inheritance, still drops you in the default company on a new machine. Worth saying out loud which one we want before building it; the first is a small addition to the account record and the `/me` payload, the second is a few lines in the provider.

## Implementation

- `company/CompanyProvider.tsx` holds the whole of the current behaviour: one `compass.companyId` key, falling back to the default company then the first company. Nothing clears it at sign-out.
- Per-account: a nullable last-company column on the user, returned with `/me` and written when the switcher changes — set-and-forget, no new screen. Falls back to the default company for an account that has never chosen, and to the default again if the remembered company has since been archived (the provider already handles the archived case and explains itself with a toast — keep that).
