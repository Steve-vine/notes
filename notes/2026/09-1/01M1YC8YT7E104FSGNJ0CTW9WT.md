## Control Objective

Callitech Limited limits the use and disclosure of PII (employee data, customer CRM records, call recordings, and customer content generated through telephone-answering, live-chat, and AI-based answering services) to purposes identified to data subjects, and discloses such information to third parties only with appropriate consent or lawful basis.

## Implementation Guidance

Permissible uses of PII collected through call handling, live chat, CRM, and AI-based answering are documented and limited to service delivery, quality assurance, and client-contracted purposes.Disclosure of PII to third parties (e.g., telecom providers, cloud hosting providers, AI tooling vendors) requires either the data subject's consent or a documented lawful basis (e.g., contractual necessity for the client relationship).Access to PII within CRM and call-recording systems is restricted by role, with approval required before data is shared outside the originating team.A log of third-party disclosures is maintained, identifying the recipient, purpose, and basis for disclosure.Vendor/sub-processor agreements (currently tracked via SharePoint/spreadsheets pending formal vendor management) document data handling and confidentiality obligations relevant to PII.Suspected unauthorized use or disclosure is investigated and handled under Callitech's incident response process.Use and disclosure practices are reviewed periodically by IT/Cybersecurity leadership, particularly as AI-based answering capabilities expand.

Consent obtained prior to disclosure

Where consent (rather than another documented lawful basis such as contractual necessity for a client engagement) is relied upon to disclose PII to a third party, the following applies:

Consent is obtained from the data subject before any disclosure is made. No disclosure is released until the consent record has been located and confirmed as current.Consent is captured in a durable, recorded form (e.g., written or emailed confirmation from the data subject, client-facing consent language for end users of telephone-answering and live-chat services, or an HR-signed acknowledgement for employee data) and identifies the data subject, the categories of PII covered, the intended recipient or category of recipient, and the purpose of the disclosure.Consent records are retained alongside the third-party disclosure log, and each log entry that relies on consent references the corresponding consent record so that the basis for each disclosure can be evidenced after the fact.Data subjects may withdraw consent by contacting the published privacy contact or the Service Desk. Withdrawals are logged in the same tracker used for data subject requests (see PRV1), and further disclosures relying on that consent cease from the point of withdrawal.Where consent is refused, withdrawn, or cannot be evidenced, disclosure is not made unless an alternative lawful basis is documented and approved by the Head of Infrastructure and Cybersecurity.
