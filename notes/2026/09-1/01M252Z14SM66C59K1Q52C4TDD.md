## Why it fired again

Consistent with the documented mechanism: `managementPolicies: ["Create", "Observe", "Delete"]` drops `LateInitialize`, so `external-name` is not repopulated after a provider pod restart; Observe then finds nothing and Create fires a duplicate.

The new duplicates cluster on **26 Aug** — the day of PRs #65/#66 (the `rate-limited-iam` DeploymentRuntimeConfig) and #67. Applying a DeploymentRuntimeConfig restarts provider pods, and every `provider-aws-ec2` restart appears to mint another connector. Worth confirming from pod restart timestamps, but the correlation is strong: 08:41 and 17:37 on 26 Aug in staging, 18:52 in production.

**The practical implication is that any provider restart is now a production-affecting event.** That raises the priority of the ASG rewrite from "tidy-up" to "this keeps costing money and will keep happening on every routine provider change".

## Cost and risk

15 unnecessary running EC2 instances across two accounts, continuously, since 26 Aug — two weeks. Per the July findings, connector tokens are per zone rather than per instance, so duplicates boot with the same token and the non-winning daemons likely cannot re-authenticate after refresh-token rotation — they are burning money without serving traffic.

## What to do

1. **Do not** clear the `external-create-pending` annotation on the stuck staging MR — the July notes are explicit that the brake is the only thing that stopped the original leak.
2. Confirm which instance each Twingate connector is actually live on (GraphQL `Connector` exposes `hostname`/`publicIP`/`privateIPs`) before terminating anything.
3. Stop rather than terminate the production orphans first, as was done in July, and soak.
4. Prioritise the ASG + LaunchTemplate rewrite from the July findings. This is the second occurrence and the first to reach production.
