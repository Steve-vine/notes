**What it says**
1. The hardest stretch is Steve 62 to 69: Lesley has stopped (2033), her 1995 pension is only £4.8k, and nothing else arrives until Steve's State Pension at 67 and hers at 69 (Steve's age). Peak draw from the pots is £40k/yr. Every year either of them keeps earning in that window is worth £15–30k of pot.
2. Stopping dead at 60 (D) does not work: pots exhausted at 76 on a £45k target; ceiling £38k. Not a plan.
3. Scenario A at £51k is feasible but only ~£6k above the £45k placeholder target, and current non-mortgage spending appears to be ~£56k including renovation. The margin is thin.
4. Overpaying the mortgage (G) is neutral: same affordable income, same pots at 90. At 3.99% it neither helps nor hurts versus investing, and it is behind pension contributions at 40–60% relief.
5. Lesley working part-time to 65 (H) adds £2k/yr of affordable income and £116k of pots at 90, and cuts the peak draw from £40k to £28k. It is the cheapest fix for the gap years.
6. Returns still dominate the CISO question: E vs F is £11k/yr; the step-up (C vs A) is £1k/yr. The concentration in MSTR and a single-region index makes the return range far wider than E to F.
