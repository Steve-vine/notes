Part of the POC Tweaks sprint. First of five, and it gates the other four: membership rules change what "a role gives you this group" means, which ADR 0061, 0063 and 0064 are built on. No code in this task.

## The problem

With many roles, a person's roles can add up to groups that contradict each other. The base role for a PA includes the group that assigns a Microsoft 365 F3 licence; a subset of PAs also hold a role whose group assigns E3. Those people get both. The only fix today is to duplicate the whole PA role without F3, and keep the two copies in step for ever.

## What Steve asked for (2026-10-13)

A set of rules, per company, each reading **If [condition] [group] then [action] [groups]**:

- Condition: **in group** or **not in group**, with one group.
- Action: **add group** or **drop group**, with one or more groups.
- Each rule can be switched on or off.
- Example: *If in group E3 then drop group F3*: where a person's roles work out to giving E3, F3 is not given even though another of their roles includes it.
- Managed in a new card, **Membership rules**, on Access Control ▸ Admin below Role mappings.

## What the record has to settle

Each with the answer proposed; Steve to confirm or change before the build tasks start.

1. **What "in group" looks at.** Proposed: what the person's roles give, as Steve described it. Not what they happen to be in in the directory, and not a group they were given individually. **Open question for Steve:** should a group given to one person individually (an approved exception) also count? If a PA is given E3 as a one-off and not through a role, the proposal leaves them with F3 as well.
2. **Who a rule applies to.** Proposed: anybody who holds at least one role in the company. Somebody with no roles is not touched, including by a "not in group" rule.
3. **Rules do not chain, and their order does not matter.** Proposed: every condition looks at what the roles give, never at what another rule did. All the adds are applied, then all the drops, and where one rule adds a group and another drops it, drop wins. So there is no ordering to manage and no rule can be changed by moving another.
4. **What "drop" can take away.** Proposed: only a group the person would otherwise get from a role or from a rule. A group somebody was given individually, or one nobody can explain, is never removed by a rule.
5. **A group a rule adds is governed like one a role gives.** Proposed: it is granted through the same requests, a leaver takes it away, a mover takes it away when the condition stops being true, and Validation does not report it as an unrequested change.
6. **A group a rule withholds is not explained by the role any more.** Proposed: somebody who is in it anyway reads as unexplained, the same as any membership no role accounts for (this amends ADR 0063's "any role the person holds maps the group").
7. **A rule change reaches the people it affects through a request.** Proposed: as a role edit does under ADR 0064. Saving, switching on, switching off or deleting a rule saves at once and raises one request covering everybody whose groups it changes, which somebody else approves. Nothing reaches the directory before that.
8. **Adds before removals.** Proposed: within one person's change, groups are added before any are removed, so somebody going from F3 to E3 is never without a licence in between.
9. **Which groups can be picked.** Proposed: the condition and "drop" take any group a role in the company gives; "add" takes any group a role could be given, with the same refusals and the same Access Admin approval for a privileged group as mapping it to a role. A dynamic group cannot be added or dropped.
10. **Rules that fight.** Proposed: the card warns when one enabled rule adds a group another drops, and refuses a rule that drops or adds its own condition group.
11. **A group that leaves the directory.** Proposed: its rule stays, is marked, and does nothing until it is repaired or deleted.
12. **Shared mailboxes are out of scope.** Rules are about groups (security groups, Microsoft 365 groups and distribution lists, as a role's group list holds them).
