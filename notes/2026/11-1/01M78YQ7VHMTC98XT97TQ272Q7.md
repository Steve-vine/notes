Plan drafted 12 Nov 2026 from Steve's brief: move from Head of Infrastructure and Cyber Security at Moneypenny (£110k) to a consultancy where he chooses his hours and winds them down later, release Compass (his GRC/compliance automation platform) free for self-hosting, and maximise income on the way. Planning view, not regulated advice. Numbers: `career/models/career-income-scenarios-v1.xlsx` (script `models/scripts/build_career_income.py`).

**The honest headline**

1. Consultancy at Steve's realistic market rate does not out-earn the job in cash. It roughly matches it in total wealth only if most of the profit goes into a pension through the limited company. The move buys hours and control, not more money. "Maximise income" therefore means: keep the £110k and its 60%-relief salary sacrifice as long as the side-line is being built, then run the consultancy through a company with heavy employer pension contributions.
2. Compass is the differentiator, not the income. Free self-hosting earns nothing by itself; it earns by making Steve the obvious person to hire, and by adding recurring hosting and support revenue that keeps paying when his billable days fall. It only works if the IP question is settled in writing first.
3. The biggest risk is not the market, it is 2029 to 2031: full-time job plus side clients plus a product launch is 270 to 280 working days a year in the model. That is the burnout window, and it is where the plan most often fails in practice.

**Positioning**

Steve's specialism is cross-discipline strategy (infrastructure, operations, SRE/DevOps, cloud, cyber) delivered hands-on. Large enterprises buy specialists; SMEs and mid-market firms (50 to 500 staff, no CISO, facing ISO 27001, SOC 2, Cyber Essentials Plus, supplier questionnaires, DORA and NIS2 pull-through from EU customers) buy exactly this generalist. So the target is fractional Head of IT/Security or vCISO for that segment, outside London, where the planning rate is £800 to £850 a day, not the £1,000 to £2,000 recruiter headline (which is regulated-sector interim CISO work, usually full-time hours). Offer retainers (£2k to £4k a month for a fixed number of days plus Compass) rather than day-rate work: smoother income and hours Steve controls.

Evidence, Nov 2026: Barclay Simpson contract Head of Information Security £700 to £900/day; recruiter guides £1,000 to £2,000/day for fractional CISO; ITJobsWatch CISO contract median £575/day outside London (six months to Oct 2026); IIM 2026 interim survey average £907/day, £949 outside IR35.

**Compass: what "free" should mean**

- Model: open-core. Free self-hosted Community edition (AGPL, so nobody can host it as a service without contributing or paying); paid managed hosting; support subscription; a paid tier for the things organisations pay for (SSO, multi-company, connectors, the AI assistant). This is the CISO Assistant and Eramba pattern, both of which survive on it. Compass's edge over them is the breadth (access control, vendors, continuity, incidents, DSRs) and the assistant.
- Market gap is real: Vanta and Drata run $20k to $35k a year for an SME and neither is self-hostable. A credible free alternative with a UK consultant behind it has a niche.
- Free also creates a support burden that eats the hours Steve wants back. Decide the support policy before release (community only, no SLA, paid support for anything with a deadline).
- Name: "Compass" clashes with Atlassian Compass and others. Trademark check, and probably a distinctive brand in front of the generic word.
- **IP (blocking).** Under s.11(2) Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 the employer is first owner of work made in the course of employment, and Penhallurick v MD5 [2021] EWHC 293 (IPEC) held that code written at home, in the employee's own time, on his own computer still belonged to the employer because it was within the job. Steve is Head of Infrastructure and Cyber Security and Compass governs infrastructure and cyber security; a court could well read it the same way whatever the employment contract says about outside work. Nothing is published, and no company is built on Compass, until Moneypenny has confirmed in writing that Steve owns it (or assigns it to him, or licenses it back with freedom to commercialise). An hour with an IP/employment solicitor first (£500 to £1,500). This is the first task of the plan and it is a hard gate.

**Phases**

*Phase A, now to mid-2028 (55 to 57): build while employed.*
- Keep the job. Raise salary sacrifice by ~£1,000/month (CAR-16): 60% relief on the first £1,200 and 42% above, far better than any return from the side-line.
- Settle the IP (Q1 2027). Choose licence, name, support policy. Public release of Compass v1 by Sept 2027 with docs, a demo instance and a hosted option.
- Form the limited company (2027), accountant, PI/PL insurance. Check the Moneypenny outside-work clause and keep it visibly clean: no Moneypenny time, kit or clients.
- Profile: LinkedIn rewritten around "resilient and secure holistic IT", two talks or articles a quarter, ISO 27001 Lead Implementer (cheap, useful for GRC work). CISSP only if a target client or recruiter asks for it.
- First paid side engagement by June 2027; two retainer clients or £15k/yr by end 2028. Cap side work at ~40 billable days a year while employed.
- Dates in play: mortgage fix ends July 2028 (CAR-15); James 18 July 2028.

*Phase B, mid-2028 to Jan 2031 (57 to 60): prove it, then choose the glide path.*
- Side income £15k to £40k gross a year, left in the company or paid into the pension (annual allowance £60k shared with the workplace scheme; carry-forward available).
- Decision point Oct 2029 (58). Three routes, all modelled:
  - S2: stay full-time to 60, leave March 2031, consultancy from then.
  - S4: negotiate 3 days a week at Moneypenny from 2029/30 for about four years, consulting alongside; full consultancy from 63. Moneypenny stays an employer, not a client (sidesteps IR35, keeps the pension and death-in-service).
  - S5: leave at 58. Modelled; it costs about £13k a year of net cash in 59 to 60 and gains nothing by 65.
- A CISO step-up at a bigger employer is not in this plan. Projection v1 showed it adds £1k/yr of affordable retirement income, and it conflicts directly with building Compass and the client base. Revisit only if by Oct 2029 the side-line has no paying clients.

*Phase C, 2031 to 2036 (60 to 65): consultancy at chosen hours.*
- 2031 to 2033: ~130 billable days (3 days a week equivalent) plus Compass; 2033 to 2036: 100 falling to 60; beyond 65: 40 or whatever Steve wants.
- Lesley retires March 2033; mortgage ends July 2033. Projection v1 says 62 to 69 is the hard stretch and each year of earned income in it is worth £15k to £30k of pot. Consultancy at 60 to 100 days a year is precisely the fix.
- Pension: do not take taxable pension income flexibly while the company is contributing, or the £10k money purchase annual allowance kills the main tax lever. Tax-free cash alone does not trigger it.

**Income levers, in order of value**

1. Salary sacrifice while employed (60% relief band). Already recommended; do it now.
2. Employer pension contributions from the company at 57 to 65: no corporation tax, no NI, no income tax on the way in. The whole case for a limited company rather than sole trading.
3. Retainers over day rates; Compass managed hosting as recurring revenue that survives fewer days.
4. Salary at the personal allowance plus dividends; consider Lesley as shareholder with professional advice.
5. Never take flexible pension income while contributing (MPAA).

**Risks and sense-checks**

- IP ownership (above). Hard gate.
- Age bias in the contract market is real; the mid-market retainer route and the product are the defence, the recruiter-led interim market is not the plan.
- Anchor-client concentration: if Moneypenny becomes the first client, cap it at half of revenue by year two.
- Rates are falling in ad data (ITJobsWatch medians down year on year); S3 is the honest floor.
- The 2029 to 2031 workload. If the side-line cannot be run inside ~8 hours a week, choose S4 (3 days a week) earlier rather than pushing through.
- Investment concentration (CAR-14) still dwarfs all of this: a bad MSTR year costs more than the whole ten-year difference between S1 and S2.
- Health and Lesley's view on the hours: not in the model, decisive in reality.

**Decisions for Steve**

1. Is "work the hours I want" worth about £9k a year less cash in hand at 61 to 66, in exchange for ~100 fewer working days a year and a larger pension? (The model says that is the trade.)
2. S2 or S4 as the default glide path (can be left until Oct 2029, but S4 needs the conversation with Moneypenny a year earlier).
3. Commission the IP check now.
