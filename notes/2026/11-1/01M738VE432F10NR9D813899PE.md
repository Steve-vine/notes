**Why.** This applies the rules in COM-1682 to 18 criteria. Today they have 85 links to 47 controls, against 58 points of focus (AICPA 2022, from COM-1683; Carbide holds none for privacy).

**Method.** As in COM-1684. The output goes in `audit/soc2-exact-set.md`.

**Controller and processor points.** Each privacy point is tagged as likely relevant to a data controller `[C]` or a data processor `[P]`. Moneypenny is likely both: a controller for its own staff and customers, a processor for its clients' callers. Cover every point either way, and keep the tag in the table so it's visible which role each control serves.

**Known items to settle here** (from the 2026-11-10 review):
- These are each rated as doing the whole job but don't:
  - P1.1 PDH.8: says nothing about telling people when the privacy notice changes, a point in its own right in 2022 ("Communicates Changes to Notice"). This is a likely gap.
  - P2.1 PDH.2.
  - P4.1 PDH.13: lists the purposes but doesn't limit use to them.
  - P8.1 PDH.17: covers complaints only, with no periodic compliance monitoring.
- ACC.14 under P6.1 is weak.
- The gap report `audit/soc2-mapping.md` names these privacy controls by their internal key, not the reference on screen: PDH.10, PDH.4, s42.PDH.2, s42.PDH.6 and ACC.5. Correct it while here. The references above are the ones on screen.
- `test_privacy_rights_control` currently requires the privacy-rights control on P2.1, P4.1 and P5.1. If this review drops any of those links, the test changes in COM-1691.
