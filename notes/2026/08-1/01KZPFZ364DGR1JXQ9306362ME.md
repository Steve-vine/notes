Found 2026-08-10. One DataDog synthetic test — Kora (UK) — produces **two incidents every time it fires**, one per triggering group (`total` and `pl:staging-uk-cluster-…`). Merging them is a human act, and it has to be repeated on every cycle.

**The split is deliberate and usually right.** `connectors/datadog.py:11` reads monitors per group so "a multi-alert monitor splits per triggering scope", keyed `monitor/{id}/{group}` — for a host-scoped monitor firing on five hosts, five incidents is the correct answer. It is wrong for a synthetic test whose groups (`total`, per private location) are views of one failure, not distinct scopes.

**The merge proposal works.** `propose_merges` falls back to `unresolved_entity_match` for an entity-less alert, which matches on the same alert source — `alert_source` strips the group, so the two Kora incidents propose each other with reason "same alert source". The desk is offered the merge correctly.

**Nothing remembers the answer.** Merging sets `master_id` on the child incident, and that is all. When the signal clears and re-fires — or when a close releases the correlation key and fresh incidents open ([ISE-646]) — two brand-new incidents appear and the same merge must be made again by hand. On staging that has already happened: the pair merged in the past are now two unmerged open incidents raised at 16:30 today.

The estate learns edges, tag mappings and playbook efficacy from what operators do. It does not learn "these two signals are always one incident", which is among the cheapest and most repetitive judgements the desk makes.

**The design question**: where does the memory live?

1. **On the pair of signals** — a remembered "these correlate" that auto-merges on the next promotion. Strongest, and closest to how `proposals` already strengthens a repeated claim.
2. **On the monitor** — a per-source rule saying its groups are one incident, which is really a statement about the *monitor's shape* and might be better read from DataDog (a synthetics monitor's groups are locations of one test).
3. **At promotion** — collapse groups of the same monitor into one incident when the groups are locations rather than scopes. Narrowest, but needs a rule for telling those apart, and reverses an ISE-116 decision that was made for good reasons.

Option 1 generalises past DataDog and past this shape; option 3 fixes this case only. Worth settling before building, since they land in different layers.

**Acceptance**: an operator who merges two incidents once is not asked to merge their successors; or the two never split in the first place, with the reason recorded.
