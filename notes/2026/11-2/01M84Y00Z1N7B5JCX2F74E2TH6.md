ADR 0169 §11 and Consequences.

**Chain:** independent (production infrastructure; no Compass code).

**Why**

Production's evidence, its scheduled Postgres backups (`postgres-backups/`) and these new copies all sit in one bucket, and the app's role can delete from it. Losing that bucket, or an attacker holding that role, takes out all three. Until this is done, "recover from a complete failure" does not cover losing the bucket.

**Steve to decide before this starts**

- **Where the second copy lives**: another AWS region and/or account. This is a data residency decision, as the copies hold personal data. A separate account protects against a compromised role; another region alone does not.

**Done when**

- New copies and scheduled backups appear in the second location, which the Compass app's role **cannot delete from**.
- A copy deleted in Compass, or past 30 days, stops being recoverable from old bucket versions within a stated time, and likewise in the second location. Erased personal data must not outlive the retention period there.
- A restore has been proved **from the second location**.
- The change and the way to check it are written down in the deployment repo.

**Technical notes**

- **Check production's actual bucket settings first.** The setup script (`examples/aws/s3-irsa.sh`) turns versioning on and adds no lifecycle rule, in which case a "deleted" object stays forever as a noncurrent version and the `compass-backups/` prefix needs a lifecycle rule. This was read from the script, not from the live bucket.
- The same script gives the role List, Get, Put and Delete on the whole bucket. Consider narrowing it, so API and worker pods cannot delete under `postgres-backups/`.
- Changes are in `devops.infrastructure.aws` / `devops.application.compass`: **Claude prepares them and Steve commits and applies.**
- Related: COM-730 (proving production's Postgres backup by restoring it, still in the backlog). Do that first or alongside it.
