A vendor or recertification account signs in and, for a moment, sees the internal Compass — the sidebar with Playbook, Posture and Admin, the company switcher, the search box, the notification bell — before it is replaced by the portal.

Nothing is clickable in that moment and nothing leaks that they could act on, but it is the one thing ADR 0040 §2 gives them a separate shell to avoid: a portal account should never be shown the internal chrome. Someone signing in for the first time sees a version of Compass they will never see again and cannot use.

## What is happening

Signing in lands everyone on `/`, and `/` sits inside the internal shell. So the shell is built — navigation, header, switcher, bell, and the requests each of those makes — and only then does the landing rule work out that this reader belongs in the portal and send them there, throwing the whole thing away.

An operator does not notice, because for them the shell it just built is the one they are about to use.

## Why it is only worth a little

Measured while investigating COM-468: the discarded shell is about 145ms of the 700ms that signing in costs a portal user on an idle machine — so fixing it makes signing in perceptibly quicker for them as well as tidier.

Against that, this is the authenticated routing tree, and the last change near it (COM-203) produced an endless refetch loop that left the dashboard permanently blank. The fix is small — decide where the reader belongs before the shell is built rather than inside it — but it must not reintroduce a guard that unmounts a subtree during a background refetch. That history is the reason this is a task of its own rather than something folded into COM-468.

## Done when

A portal-only account signing in goes from the login form to the portal without the internal navigation appearing at any point, and an operator still lands on the dashboard exactly as now. The COM-203 test — "keeps the dashboard mounted instead of refetching the user forever", which counts fetches rather than trusting the text — must still pass, and it is the one that matters here.

## Origin

Found while measuring the slow tests in COM-468 (PR #452), where it was deliberately left alone rather than smuggled into a test-stability change.
