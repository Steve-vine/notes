Where the sprint earns its claim. Everything before this makes it possible; this
is the task that makes "implement every Core control and all eight frameworks
follow" true or false.

The current crosswalk was built control-first and it shows: 425 mappings, 203
distinct controls used, and **393 of the 407 covered requirements resting on
exactly one control**. Only 14 have two or more. A genuine crosswalk is
many-to-many by nature — a near-perfect 1:1 is the signature of a keyword match,
which is what the seed script says it is.

## The method

**Requirement-first, not control-first.** For each requirement in all eight
frameworks, ask: which set of Core controls, taken together, satisfies this? Then
record each with its relationship and strength, and mark the requirement jointly
complete when the set actually closes it.

That inversion is the whole point. Going control-first produces exactly what
exists now — one plausible match per requirement and a stop.

Worked examples of what needs to change, from the review:

- SOC 2 `CC6.1` → `ACC.2` alone today ("an access control policy has been
  established"). The criterion covers identification, authentication,
  authorisation, credential protection, network access and encryption of
  protected assets. Needs a set.
- ISO `A.5.3` → `CHM.7` alone (build versus deploy). Organisational segregation
  of duties is a different control, now written.
- PCI `8.3` → `CRD.2` alone (password policy). Also factor types, credential
  storage and transmission, change and history, lockout, initial credentials.
- PCI `12.8` → `VEM.1` alone. Six sub-requirements against one "a vendor
  management process exists".
- ISO `A.8.24` → `KMC.3` alone (key lifetime). The cryptography policy and
  approved algorithms are separate controls.
- ISO `A.5.34` → `PDH.3` alone (lawful basis). Identification and protection of
  PII is broader.

## Also in scope

- **Triage the orphans.** 66 Core controls map to nothing. Some are genuinely
  company-specific and fine to keep; most are mapping gaps — `ACC.8` access
  recertification, `EVM.2` privileged activity logging and `NES.4` encryption in
  transit all plainly satisfy requirements currently marked unmapped. Expect
  around 45 to map on inspection. Free coverage.
- **Re-map at the new granularity.** PCI moves to `x.y.z` and HIPAA gains nested
  standards, so those mappings are rebuilt rather than migrated. Cyber Essentials
  Danzell needs fresh mappings, especially `UAC5`, whose meaning changed.
- **Map the ISO 27001 clauses** to the new governance controls.
- **Map ISO 42001** (COM-430), and expect it to land partly covered — that is the
  intended outcome, not a failure. Two cautions: its clauses 4–10 mirror 27001's
  in structure but not in subject, so map both without claiming an ISMS
  management review discharges an AIMS one; and record honestly where the AI
  controls simply do not exist yet. The resulting list is the input to the
  decision about an AI control domain, so its accuracy matters more than its
  size.

## Implementation

- Depends on every framework-library task and every control task. It is last for
  a reason: mapping against a set that is still moving is wasted work.
- Expect the mapping count to roughly **triple, 425 → around 1,200**, before
  42001 adds its own. That is the shape of a real crosswalk.
- Work framework by framework, each as its own PR. Eight reviewable diffs beats
  one unreviewable one.
- The seed CSVs are regenerated from the final state, not hand-edited alongside
  it — one source of truth, and the importer stays idempotent.
- Record *why* for anything non-obvious. The `note` field exists; a mapping
  someone will question in an audit should already carry its answer.

Tests: no requirement is left with only `intersects_with` mappings and a
`coverage_complete` flag set; every framework's coverage is reproducible from the
CSVs; the orphan count is stated in the PR, not silently reduced.
