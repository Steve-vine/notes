Every register scrolls as one page, so the moment you move down a list you lose the things that tell you what you are reading: the screen name, the tab bar, the filters you set, and the column headers. Row 40 is a row of values with nothing naming them. Paging is worse — "Next" sits below the last row, so reaching it means scrolling past everything you just read.

Decided 2026-08-30: on a register, the head and the footer stay put and only the rows move.

## What changes for the reader

On every register — Vendors, Risks, Gaps, Controls, Domains, Frameworks, Decisions, Content, Activity, Actions, the Access Control tabs (Role matrix, Requests, Validation, Recertification, Coverage, Reports, Groups, Users, Devices, Directory Roles, Conditional Access) and the portal's lists — the screen title, the tab bar, the filter row and the column headers are fixed at the top; the paging controls are fixed at the bottom; the rows scroll between them.

The browser's own scrollbar leaves those screens. The list carries its own.

## The three questions this settles

- **The paging footer freezes.** Today you scroll to the end of 50 rows to reach "Next", and the count of what you are looking at goes with it.
- **The tab bar freezes with the header.** On Access Control, Vendors, Risks, Admin and Content the frozen head is title + tab bar + filters + column headers, in that order. The alternative — a tab bar that scrolls away while its own column headers stay — reads as a bug.
- **Group headings are left alone** for now. In a grouped table (the role matrix) they scroll with the rows. Sticking them as well is a second task if it turns out to be wanted.

## Not in scope

Detail pages — a control, a vendor, a risk, a decision, a campaign. These are documents, and freezing the top of a document is lost reading space, not orientation. They keep scrolling as they do.

## What it costs

Once a screen cannot scroll as a whole, the head is charged against the rows. On a 13" laptop a register with a title, a wrapped tab bar and a filter row shows noticeably fewer rows than it does today — Access Control's thirteen tabs wrap to two or three lines on a narrow window. That is the real cost of the change and the thing to look at on staging before the sweep goes further than the first few screens.

It also has to land as **one sweep**. Half the site freezing its head and half not is worse than either.

## Notes

- The shell change is one place: `components/AppLayout.tsx`. `AppShell.Main` becomes a fixed-height flex column — `100dvh` less the header, `overflow: hidden` — rather than a region that grows. `dvh`, not `vh`, or the bottom of the list hides under mobile browser chrome.
- The per-screen change is a shared wrapper (head / scrolling body / footer) that each register uses. Every register is already `Title → filters → Table → paging` inside one `<Stack gap="md">`, so the edit is moving three lines and an import. Roughly 20 screens.
- Column headers come free from Mantine's `stickyHeader` on `Table` once the table is inside a scroll pane — they stick to the top of that pane, so no offset arithmetic against the header height.
- **The window no longer scrolls, so nothing resets scroll on navigation.** Opening a detail page from row 200 of a list would land you part-way down it. The change needs an explicit scroll-to-top on route change; there is none today (nothing in the tree calls `scrollTo`).
- `RestoredCompanyNotice` and `RouteErrorBoundary` sit above the routed page inside `AppShell.Main` — decide whether the notice pins above the frozen head or scrolls with the page it annotates.
- Assessments is the one special case: since COM-527 it is a queue beside a panel, and the two need to scroll independently under one frozen head.
- Admin's config tables are not registers. They stay as they are, scrolling inside the pane beneath the frozen title and tab bar.
- Tests are largely unaffected — jsdom has no layout engine, so nothing asserting on rows or headers cares. Add one or two pinning the wrapper's structure so a later screen does not quietly revert to the old shape.
- Record the outcome in `brief/information-architecture.md` → *Screen conventions*. It is a rule about how a screen is dressed, so it belongs there and not in an ADR.
