The 63 stored sub-requirements are correct and complete for all 12 requirements
at the `x.y` level. The problem is that `x.y` is not where PCI is assessed.

A ROC or SAQ is completed at `x.y.z` — roughly 280 defined requirements. At
`x.y`, "Requirement 8.3 — strong authentication established and managed" is a
single met-or-not answer standing in for factor types, credential storage and
transmission, password change and history, lockout, and initial credential
handling. Nobody can evidence that, and an assessor would not accept it.

It also hides the thing most likely to fail an assessment right now: the **51
future-dated requirements** introduced in v4.x became mandatory on **31 March
2025**. Every one of them lives at `x.y.z`. Compass currently cannot show which
have been adopted.

One more consequence worth naming. Fourteen of the 63 rows are the `x.1`
"processes and mechanisms for … are defined and understood" requirements, and
**every one of them is unmapped**. That is not an oversight in the crosswalk —
there is no Core control that says "this policy is documented, current, in use,
and assigned to an owner", which is exactly what those requirements test. The
governance controls will fix it; noting it here so the mapping pass expects them.

## What changes for the reader

PCI reads as the standard is structured: 12 requirements, sub-requirements, and
the defined requirements beneath them, with coverage rolling up.

Future-dated requirements are marked, so a company can see at a glance whether
it has caught up with the March 2025 deadline.

**Expect PCI's coverage figure to fall further than any other framework's.** At
`x.y`, requirement 8.3 mapped to one control and read as covered; at `x.y.z` it
becomes eleven requirements, most of them partly covered. That is specificity
working correctly, but it will look like a regression to anyone who has not been
told. Say so on the framework page when the number moves.

## What the granularity change costs in Core controls

Less than it looks — roughly **6–10 new controls**, not fifty. Most of the ~280
defined requirements are the same obligations the library already carries, with
a number attached: password length, log retention, scan frequency, session
timeout, rotation periods. Those need no new control. They belong in the
**description** ("what good looks like" states the framework's number, per
COM-417) and in the crosswalk as several `subset_of` mappings against one
control.

What is genuinely absent — verified against the library, which contains **no**
control mentioning masking, truncation, tokenisation, cryptoperiods, key
custodians, split knowledge, control failure, or compliance scope:

- **Account data handling** (Req 3.2–3.5) — storage minimised, authentication
  data not retained after authorisation, data rendered unreadable wherever
  stored, masked on display and restricted from copy. → COM-427, Information
  Classification.
- **Key management depth** (Req 3.6–3.7) — cryptoperiods and rotation, key
  custodian acknowledgements, split knowledge and dual control. → COM-426,
  Cryptography.
- **Payment page integrity** (Req 6.4.3, 11.6) — script inventory, authorisation
  and change detection. → COM-426, Secure Development.
- **Point-of-interaction devices** (Req 9.5) — inventory and periodic tamper
  inspection. → COM-426, Physical.
- **Detection of control failure** (Req 10.7) — knowing within a defined window
  that logging or anti-malware has stopped working. Not PCI-specific in spirit,
  and absent today. → COM-427, Logging & Detection.
- **Scope definition and validation** (Req 12.5) — already covered by the
  compliance-programme control in COM-425.

## Sector-specific controls in a company-agnostic library

Several of the above are meaningless to a company that takes no cards. Add them
anyway and let **applicability** carry it — ADR 0011 already gives every Core
control a per-company applicability decision with a justification, and COM-415
adds the same at requirement level.

Keeping them out would break ADR 0010's "Core is the superset" and leave
companies that *do* take cards permanently short. HIPAA has exactly this shape
with ePHI, and any sector framework added later will too. This is the first time
the library knowingly carries controls most companies will mark not applicable —
be deliberate about it rather than surprised by it.

## Implementation

- Re-import at `x.y.z` and keep `x.y` as a parent, using the requirement self-FK
  and the `assessable` flag from COM-421. Both PCI levels **are** assessable —
  unlike ISO 42001's control objectives — so set the flag accordingly.
- v4.0.1 is the only active version (v4.0 retired 31 December 2024), so no
  supersede chain is needed yet. Set `effective_from` anyway.
- `future_dated` flag with its effective date. It is historic now, but a reader
  looking at an unmet future-dated requirement should know it is already
  overdue rather than upcoming.
- Requirement text is copyrighted — refs and titles only, descriptions enriched
  in-app (ADR 0028).
- Existing `x.y` mappings do not carry down automatically. Re-map at `x.y.z` in
  the crosswalk rebuild; leaving the parent mappings in place would claim
  coverage that was never assessed.
- **PCI is the largest single chunk of COM-428** — mapping 280 requirements
  requirement-first is more work than the other seven frameworks' increments
  combined. If that task needs splitting, PCI is the natural seam.

Tests: coverage rolls from `x.y.z` to `x.y` to requirement; a future-dated
requirement is visibly distinct; no orphaned `x.y` mappings survive the import.
