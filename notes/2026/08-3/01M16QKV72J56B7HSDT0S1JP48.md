**Observed on staging, 2026-08-29.** A membership granted through Compass's own access request shows as *Unexplained*. It is not one membership:

```
select provenance, count(*) from governed_memberships group by 1;
 unattributed | 45324
```

**Zero explained. Every membership in the system is unattributed** — including the ones Compass granted itself and can prove it granted.

## The chain

1. **10:30:57Z** — the access request executes. `tasks/access_execute.py:251` calls `membership_provenance.record(...)`, writing a `governed_memberships` row with `provenance = request` and `request_id` set. Correct at this point.
2. **10:45Z** — the next sync pass runs `membership_provenance.reconcile()`. It is **deliberately unscoped** (`core/membership_provenance.py:120-139`, COM-480) and computes `stale = recorded - live`, deleting every recorded pair the mirror does not hold. The mirror does not hold this membership yet — that is [[COM-508]]. **The attribution row is deleted.**
3. **12:17:29Z** — the full crawl finally mirrors the membership. `reconcile()` now sees it in `live` but not in `recorded`, so it creates it fresh as `unattributed`.

Proof it was deleted rather than merely never written: the surviving row's `created_at` is `2026-08-29 12:17:29.705496+00`, the full-crawl timestamp, not the 10:30:57 execution. `record()` is an upsert, so had the original survived, reconcile would have left it alone.

The evidence trail is intact and unused — `access_changes` holds `change_kind = member_added`, `request_id = c429b365-…`, `applied_at = 2026-08-29 10:30:57.257798+00`. Compass knows exactly why Natasha Berlinski is in that group. It just deleted the row that said so, and never looks at the ledger again.

## Why this matters more than the sync delay

[[COM-508]] makes access data *late*. This makes the record of **why** someone has access **permanently wrong**, and no later sync repairs it — the request link is gone for good. Coverage, membership provenance and the "unexplained access" backlog are the features this feeds; at 0/45,324 they are reporting that nothing in the tenant is governed, which is both false and unactionable.

## Fix

`reconcile()` treats "the mirror has not caught up" as "the membership does not exist". Two parts:

1. **Stop deleting fresh records.** A grace period on `attributed_at` — do not treat a record younger than a sync interval or two as stale. Cheapest change, closes the window that [[COM-508]] opens wide and that would still exist as a race even once the mirror is prompt (an Entra write landing mid-pass).
2. **Make attribution recoverable, not a one-shot side-write.** When `reconcile()` creates a row for a membership new to the record, look for a matching `access_changes` row (`member_added` / `group_created`, by `directory_user_id` + `group_id`) and attribute it from the ledger. Attribution then survives any mirror lag, and — importantly — **this is what repairs the existing 45,324**, since the ledger still holds the history.

Note COM-480 made `reconcile()` unscoped deliberately, and for a good reason recorded in its docstring. Do not re-scope it as the fix.

## Verify
Grant access through a request, then confirm the membership reads as explained *both* immediately and after the next full crawl. Also confirm a membership removed in Entra loses its record, so the grace period doesn't resurrect dead access.
